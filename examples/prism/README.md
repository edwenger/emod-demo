# PRISM Uganda Malaria Cohort Simulation

This directory contains analyses comparing EMOD within-host malaria simulations to observed data from the PRISM cohort study in Uganda (Nagongera site, high transmission).

## Overview

The PRISM study followed households in three Ugandan sites with different transmission intensities. We focus on **Nagongera (Tororo district)** - a high transmission setting - to calibrate and validate the within-host model against detailed longitudinal data including:
- Routine scheduled visits (quarterly 2011-2014, then monthly 2014+)
- Unscheduled fever-triggered visits with treatment
- Microscopy and LAMP detection at visits
- Clinical malaria episodes with antimalarial treatment

## Key Features Implemented

### Transmission and Interventions
- **Seasonality**: Based on observed clinical malaria incidence (peak May-June rainy season, low Feb-Mar dry season)
- **IRS intervention**: 83% transmission reduction starting 2015-01-01 (reflects observed 6x fold reduction)
- **EIR calibration**: Monthly EIR = 0.5 (calibrated to pre-IRS clinical episode incidence)

### Treatment and Healthcare Access
- **Treatment coverage**: 50% during burn-in, 75% during trial period (balancing clinical incidence vs routine prevalence)
- **Treatment delay**: 3-day exponential mean delay between fever onset and clinic visit (allows densities to grow)
- **Fever threshold**: 38.5°C triggers treatment-seeking (ComplexTreatment from seasonal_challenge.py)
- **Minimum episode interval**: 14 days between clinical episodes

### Detection Methods
- **Microscopy**: Probabilistic Poisson sampling model examining 1/20 μL blood volume
  - At 32 parasites/μL: 80% detection
  - At 16 parasites/μL: 55% detection
  - Hard cutoff at <8 parasites/μL (lowest observed value)
- **LAMP**: Deterministic threshold at 2 parasites/μL (done only on microscopy negatives)
- **Gametocytes**: Same probabilistic model as asexual parasites

### Study Design
- **Representative cohort**: 43 individuals sampled across age bins (<1, 1-2, 2-5, 5-10, 10-20, 20+)
  - 8 individuals per bin (except 10-20 with only 3 available)
- **Visit schedule**: Matches observed quarterly→monthly transition in Dec 2014
- **Burn-in**: Age-appropriate immunity via simulation from birth to enrollment

### Simulation
**`07_household_simulation.py`** - Main simulation matching PRISM protocol
- **Key parameters**:
  - `MONTHLY_EIR = 0.5` (calibrated to pre-IRS incidence)
  - `TREATMENT_COVERAGE_TRIAL = 0.75` (balancing incidence vs prevalence)
  - `TREATMENT_DELAY_MEAN_DAYS = 3.0` (exponential distribution)
  - `MICROSCOPY_SAMPLE_VOLUME_FRACTION = 1/20` (Poisson detection model)
- **Outputs**: Visit records, daily densities, household plots
- **Runtime**: ~5 minutes for 43 individuals over 5.4 years

- Clinical episode incidence (episodes/person-year)
- Routine visit prevalence

**`10_calibration_diagnostics.py`** - Calibration targets with individual variation
- **Filters to pre-IRS period (before 2015)** for cleaner calibration
- Distribution of clinical episodes per child (histograms showing variation, not just means)
- Age-specific incidence with error bars (SEM)
- Routine visit prevalence by age
- **Key outputs**:
  - `figures/calibration_episode_distribution.png` - Shows individual-level variation
  - `figures/calibration_age_incidence.png` - Mean incidence with uncertainty
  - `figures/calibration_routine_prevalence.png` - Prevalence and density patterns

**`11_clinical_vs_routine_correlation.py`** - Structural model limitation diagnostic
- **Key question**: Do individuals with more clinical episodes also have more routine positives?
- Scatter plots by age group showing correlation (or lack thereof)
- **Observed pattern**: Positive correlation (r=0.3-0.4 in ages 1-10) - high-exposure individuals get both
- **Simulated pattern**: Weak/absent correlation in young children - suggests max_infections constraint blocks new incidents when chronic carriage present
- **Output**: `figures/clinical_vs_routine_correlation.png`

## Known Model Limitations

### 1. "Knife-Edge" Calibration Problem
**Issue**: Cannot simultaneously match clinical incidence and routine prevalence
- **At 90% treatment**: Clinical incidence too high, routine prevalence too high (chronic carriage)
- **At 75% treatment**: Clinical incidence good (2-5 years), but routine prevalence too low
- **Root cause**: Model lacks realistic infection-to-infection variation in symptom presentation
- **Impact**: Must choose which metric to prioritize (we chose clinical incidence)

### 2. Max Infections Constraint (max_infections=5)
**Issue**: Chronic carriage artificially blocks new incident infections
- **Evidence**: Clinical vs routine correlation analysis (script 11)
  - Observed: r=0.3-0.4 positive correlation (high-exposure → both clinical + routine positives)
  - Simulated: r≈0 no correlation in ages 1-10 (chronic carriage blocks new incidents)
- **Mechanism**: When 5 chronic strains present, new challenges cannot establish → fewer clinical episodes
- **Reality**: High-exposure individuals should have both more chronic carriage AND more incident episodes
- **Impact**: Underestimates clinical incidence in individuals with high routine positivity

### 3. Infant Clinical Incidence Too High
**Issue**: <1 year olds have 4x too high clinical incidence (3.08 obs vs 12.60 sim)
- May relate to maternal antibodies or other age-specific immunity not captured in model
- Could also be artifact of small sample size (limited infants in cohort)

### 4. Treatment Delay Distribution
**Current**: Single exponential distribution (mean 3 days)
- **Captures**: Realistic density growth before treatment
- **Missing**: Variation by severity, distance to clinic, socioeconomic factors
- **Impact**: Simulated max densities still lower than observed (92K vs 700K)

## Major Insights

1. **Treatment delay is critical** - Instantaneous treatment produced max densities of 19K; 3-day delay increased to 92K (5x improvement)

2. **Age stratification matters** - Clinical episodes in adults (median 800 /μL) vs young children (median 22,680 /μL) have very different density profiles

3. **Pre-IRS calibration is cleaner** - Focusing on 2011-2014 period removes confounding from IRS intervention

4. **Individual variation is important** - Distributions show wide range (some children 10+ episodes/year, others 1/year), not just high averages

5. **Probabilistic detection is realistic** - Poisson sampling with 1/20 μL volume matches observed low-density detection patterns better than deterministic thresholds

## Recommendations for Future Work

### Near-term Improvements
1. **Increase max_infections** to 8-10 to reduce artificial blocking of new challenges
2. **Add heterogeneous biting** - some individuals get more exposure (would help correlation pattern)
3. **Tune EIR** down slightly to reduce <1 year incidence
4. **Explore treatment coverage** in 70-80% range with larger parameter sweep

### Structural Model Enhancements
1. **Infection-to-infection variation** in symptom likelihood (not all infections→fever at same threshold)
2. **Maternal immunity** for infants
3. **Heterogeneous treatment-seeking delay** (distribution of delays, not single exponential)
4. **Superinfection dynamics** - allow new strains even when at max_infections

### Additional Analyses
1. **Parameter sweep** over EIR × treatment coverage grid
2. **Walukuba and Kihihi sites** (medium and low transmission) for broader validation
3. **Post-IRS period** validation (2015-2016)
4. **Gametocyte dynamics** (currently 3-7x too high prevalence)

python 07_household_simulation.py

python 08_compare_simulated_vs_observed.py  # Main comparison
